New in this playtest 38.43 Construct III Playtest: Construct III can draw without building View changes
  • Construct III can draw without building: Play Construct III to draw one card without building, or build normally and draw once. Rivals use the draw-only choice and prefer Construct III over equivalent Construct II orders. Construct III.
  • Draw your next hand at cleanup: You now draw your opening five cards at setup, and each later hand at the end of the round, after everyone finishes Buy and nobody has won. The round reads Production, Operations, Buy, Cleanup & draw, so you can plan your next hand while the round wraps up. A round of play.
  • A real table that fits your window: On a desktop window the game no longer scrolls: the whole window is one table, scaled as a whole to fit. The map fills all the space the other pieces leave, in any window shape. In its top-left corner a small printed track races every faction to the Prestige goal (the acting faction's marker glows; point at a marker for that faction's overview, select it for the breakdown), and in its bottom-left corner a small comms card opens into the whole log. The turn and round and the whole supply at once (an Upgrades tray, the market and the alien relics) lie to the right, and your seat runs along the bottom: Deck and In play, your hand, a player mat of pips (no numbers) for production, stock and a gold capacity fence, and Discard with the turn's buttons. Drag 4 of one resource onto another on your mat to trade with the bank (Player mat). Cards look like printed card stock in a slight fan; point at one to read it whole, drag it onto a lit hex to play it or onto your mat to convert it. Market prices sit in each card's corner with a padlock on any resource above your limit, cards you can't buy yet are shaded with the reason (Buy), and held planets are ringed and buildings drawn in their owner's colour (Reading ownership). iPads get the same table, and phones a full-screen map with the hand, supply, mat, comms and track in drawers.
  • The market splits into three rows by price: The market is now three decks sorted by total price: Expensive (8 or more), Medium (6–7) and Cheap (5 or less), each with three face-up cards below the relics. A bought card is replaced from its own row's deck; when that deck runs out, the slot stays empty. Market rows.
  • Trash cards from your hand during Buy: During your Buy turn you can now also trash cards that were still in your hand when Buy began, for the usual 3 of one resource each, alongside your discard and cards in play. Cards you buy, draw or use during that Buy stay ineligible. Buy.
  • The turn lies with your seat: The table is rearranged around your seat (Shared board). The left column holds, top to bottom, the Prestige track, the round's phases, the comms log, your fleets at a glance, the Upgrades (Move II, Construct II, Add 4 capacity, Move III, Construct III and the Trash pile, each a card that lights the resources you can pay with now) and the map key with each piece's price. The map fills the middle. The right column shows the relics and the market as real cards (63:88), with the market in three rows of three from its most expensive to its cheapest cards. Along the bottom are the menu and piles, your hand as a low fanned hand, the turn prompt with small turn buttons and Trade, a large player mat and Discard. On your turn your hand glows and gently breathes instead of a "Your turn" banner. Cards read like printed cards: the name and effect start at the top-left, the cost runs down the right edge, and effects use a shared set of icons (★ Prestige, Draw, Move, Hit, Miss, resource limit, Colonize, Build), which are listed in the map key and also appear on the starter cards. Resource icons are filled. The mat no longer offers a Discard; resources above a limit are still discarded automatically (Resources). Preview all relics is in the menu. Browser zoom works: zooming in makes the table's pieces larger and leaves the map less room.
  • Keep human Undo through a release: New playtest releases retain human-turn Undo, including later bot actions until another human acts. Older tables keep their own rules. Shared-table Undo.
  • Faster rival planning: Solo computer rivals plan movement and construction faster while preserving their decisions, costs and card order. Computer rivals.
  • Rivals find another bank exchange before skipping a card: When a bank exchange would spend the funds a rival keeps for its next unit, the rival now tries exchanging a different resource before it passes on the card. Rival cards.

        QUICK REFERENCE

        How to command your faction

        Read the canonical rulebook · 38.43 ↗

        Your turn

        Convert level I starters for resources, then use one order, offer a trade, or pass. Passing immediately starts your Buy turn. Finish Buy, then the remaining factions continue Operations.

        Your round

        Produce → Operations (pass → your Buy turn) → everyone finishes → cleanup → victory check → draw five → next round.

        Your deck

        Buy as many cards as you can afford during your Buy turn. Upgrade original starters to II for two of one resource, then III for three of one resource; III draws one card when used as an order. Only level I starters convert to resources. Trash eligible discarded cards, including starters, for three of one chosen resource type each. There is no one-card trash limit. Gained cards go to discard. Played and converted cards stay in play until shared cleanup. Buy a Capacity Module from the Upgrades tray (1 of each resource, every time); play it during Operations to add 4 capacity points, and it stays in your permanent Storage pile. Successful Colonize, Excavate and Cargo Expansion are permanently trashed. Probe keeps its combined card. Stored resources carry forward.

        Your goal

        Reach the table’s Prestige target (30 unless chosen otherwise when the game was created) at the end of a round. Colonies, excavated relics, and decisive combat victories contribute. Click a point total to see its breakdown. The rulebook includes costs, scoring, and current card clarifications.
