## 39.44 · Playtest 2026-11-16

<!-- release after bb721da -->
Rules-changing release: start a new game. Unfinished 38.43 games and tables keep the archived 38.43 Construct III Playtest engine.
- **Keep the original resource mat on the green table**: The green background restores production squares and the gold capacity marker. The star background keeps the combined circle display with thick production rings; switching backgrounds updates the mat immediately. [Resources and storage](rulebook.html#resources).
- **Choose your table background**: Toggle Star background in the game menu to switch between green and stars. Your browser remembers your choice. [Background](rulebook.html#background-preference).
- **Read battles across the card table**: Enemy ships and tactics now face yours across a battle table, using the map’s familiar unit symbols, attack values and hull tracks. See both public tactic inventories, select a card, then commit your secret choice. [Combat](rulebook.html#combat-table).
- **A clearer starfield**: The optional star background has brighter stars in three sizes, more stars, and full-height coverage. [Background preference](rulebook.html#background-preference).
- **One rival policy for games and simulations** (rules change): New shared tables and AI arena matches use the same computer rivals as solo play. Existing tables retain their dealt policies. [Computer rivals](rulebook.html#computer-rivals).
- **Compare build costs in a resource table**: The map key shows ships, buildings, and excavation in rows with four resource columns. Blank cells mean zero cost. [Ships and buildings](rulebook.html#building).
- **Fleet protection and Buy exchanges** (rules change): Fleets take combat hits before Settlers, followed by Explorers and Shipyards. Play held 2:1 exchange cards during your own Buy turn as well as Operations, and keep buying afterward. The Trash pile shows its price: 3 of one resource per card. [Combat](rulebook.html#combat-tactics) · [Exchange cards](rulebook.html#exchange-orders) · [Buy](rulebook.html#buy).
- **Read battles on combat mats**: Willem’s battles use automatic attack and hull dials, units in hit order, and separate spaces for available, committed and spent tactics. Follow simultaneous reveals on the shared volley tracker and revisit the calculations in the battle report; combat rules and Max’s UI are unchanged. [Combat mats](rulebook.html#combat-table).
- **Keep Capacity Modules at a fixed price** (rules change): Every Capacity Module costs 1 Matter, 1 Energy, 1 Biomass and 1 Data. Buying or storing modules never increases the price. [Capacity Modules](rulebook.html#capacity-module).
- **Focus the Cheap market on draw and exchange cards** (rules change): Cheap now contains only the three draw-two and three 2:1 exchange designs. Ion Thrusters, Slipstream Charts, Rail Battery and Deflector Grid move to Medium. Six other former Cheap designs leave the new-game supply. Prices and effects stay the same. [Market rows](rulebook.html#market-tiers).
- **Exchange cards, market prices and goal-directed rivals** (rules change): Three cheap exchange cards let you choose a resource to trade at 2:1 until your Buy ends; eight market cards cost less and World Engine draws after its free build. New games default to 30 Prestige, and rivals improve production, purchase planning and goal-directed exchanges. [Exchange orders](rulebook.html#exchange-orders), [market cards](rulebook.html#cards), [rival planning](rulebook.html#rival-planning), [exchange planning](rulebook.html#rival-card-timing), [victory target](rulebook.html#victory-target).
- **Play against MaxBot** (rules change): Choose MaxBot in New game to replace the Economy rival. It seeks reusable capacity, develops Harvesters and Colonies with local Shipyards, and turns toward combat and Prestige purchases later. New games only. [MaxBot](rulebook.html#maxbot).
- **Military Focused hunts in five-Fleet groups** (rules change): The optional Military Focused rival settles one additional world, builds its forward Shipyard there, and hunts weaker enemy groups with five Fleets at a time. The original Military rival stays available; Harvesters, Capacity Modules, card draw, exchange cards and tactics support the new plan. [Computer rivals](rulebook.html#military-focused-rival).
- **Randomize your solo starting home** (rules change): New solo games deal all four home-world positions at random, so you can start at any corner. The setup preview names your actual neighbor, and saved games keep their dealt positions. [Solo setup](rulebook.html#solo-seating).
- **Replace enemy Harvesters on captured worlds** (rules change): After capturing a world’s Colony, use Construct and pay the normal Harvester cost to replace an enemy Harvester there with your own. Its hex and production become yours. Colonies, Shipyards and your own buildings cannot be replaced. [Building](rulebook.html#building).
- **Tiered gallery and unclaimed destroyed Shipyards** (rules change): Browse the card gallery by Expensive, Medium and Cheap tiers, with sorting and deck filters inside each tier. Destroying a Shipyard now clears ownership of its hex, allowing a normal paid claim and build there again. [Market tiers](rulebook.html#market-tiers) · [Destroyed Shipyards](rulebook.html#destroyed-shipyards).
- **Members dashboard for hosts**: Administrators get a **Members** page beside Invitations: every invitation link with how many people accepted it and who, and every registered account with how they joined, when they were last seen and how many tables they have. Players see no change.
- **Capacity is now a card** (rules change): **Add 4 capacity** is now the **Capacity Module**, a card you buy from the Upgrades tray during Buy for 1 + the cards in your Storage pile, of each resource (the table shares 20). It enters your discard like any purchase; play it during Operations as your action to add 4 capacity points where you choose, and it goes to your permanent Storage pile beside your mat. You can no longer buy capacity off turn. [Resources & storage](rulebook.html#storage-allocation).
- **Card art from the National Gallery of Art**: Every card now shows a public-domain painting, print or photograph from the National Gallery of Art, Washington: Romantic seascapes and landscapes for market and starter cards, naval battles for the military deck, and Piranesi engravings and star charts for the alien relics. On the table a market card's picture fills the space its text leaves. The card gallery lists every work and its artist. The art never changes what a card does ([Card reference](rulebook.html#card-art)). The game menu, rulebook and card gallery end with the version and a short art notice.
- **Read a card from its corners**: Every card shows what it does as symbols in its upper-left corner (one hammer on Construct, two on Construct II) and, in the upper-right corner, what a level I starter converts to or a market card's price. In your hand those corners are the card's buttons: press the symbols to use the order, the resource to convert. Your hand is a fan in which both corners of every card show; point at a card to raise it whole. [Read a card from its corners](rulebook.html#card-corners).
- **A larger map key with prices beside their names**: The map key's names and card icons are larger, each price sits just after its name, faint lines part the rows, and each resource's symbols form one cluster: two side by side, three as a small triangle. On an upright tablet the costs are in the Map key, not on the table. [Shared board](rulebook.html#shared-board).
- **Bigger card corners that look like buttons**: Every card in your hand shows its two corner buttons as big tabs set into the card's corners, about a third of the card's width, resting in the fan or raised. The order corner is tinted, the convert corner is in its resource's colour, and each has a raised edge that flattens when pressed. When a corner's action is not open (a rival's turn, another phase) its tab is dashed and dimmed but still shows what the card does. A name that no longer fits between a raised card's corners sits under them, and a resting card's name wraps onto two lines or gets a little smaller instead of being cut off. No resting card shows text cut short with "…": a rules line shows only when it fits whole, and in a hand too full for a name, a card without corner symbols shows its picture instead. On a touch screen the first tap on a resting card raises it and the next press acts. [Read a card from its corners](rulebook.html#card-corners), [Orders](rulebook.html#orders).
- **The site is now Heirs Ad Astra**: The home page, account, tables, AI arena, invitations and sign-in now carry the Heirs Ad Astra name, and the home page opens on Heirs Ad Astra. The other experimental game is no longer offered; your Heirs Ad Astra tables, accounts, AI players and replays are unchanged.
- **Turn Guidance on or off from the table**: A **Guidance** switch now sits just above Map key (on a phone, first among the turn's buttons): one click turns the notes off or back on, and it always matches Guidance in the menu. [Guidance](rulebook.html#guidance).
- **Introducing Heirs Ad Astra**: The game, its home page, rulebook, card gallery, tables, AI arena, invitations and sign-in now carry the name Heirs Ad Astra. Play it at [heirsadastra.app](https://heirsadastra.app/). Your saved tables, accounts, AI players and replays are unchanged.
- **New resource colours and the Heirs Ad Astra emblem**: Resources have new colours everywhere they appear: Matter is amber, Energy coral, Life mint and Data cyan. On cards each resource is a small chip in its colour. The home page and the browser tab show the Heirs Ad Astra emblem, a hanging astrolabe whose speed lines carry the four resource colours.
- **The map shows what you can play on a hex**: Point at a hex (or focus it with Tab) and the cards in your hand that could start an order there rise and glow. Select the hex to open its Sector Scan as a note beside it, its arrow aimed at the hex. It lists those orders with each card's picture; choosing one starts the order as Use order or a drag would. Escape or a click elsewhere closes it. [Your basic orders](rulebook.html#orders).
- **The home page shows the emblem**: The large picture on the home page is now the Heirs Ad Astra emblem, in place of the orbit drawing. On a phone held upright it sits beside the game's name.
- **The market opens from the table's edge**: The supply (relics, market and Upgrades) is now a drawer on the table's right edge, closed until you open it with the **Market** tab, so the map has its room. Your Buy turn brings the market forward as whole cards with their pictures, and afterwards it goes back to how you had it; on a phone the Supply drawer does the same. [Shared board](rulebook.html#shared-board).
- **Phones are played sideways**: On a phone, hold it sideways to play: the map fills the screen, your hand lies along the bottom as a fan with the turn's buttons at its right end (narrow: **Pass &** over **buy**, and **Convert all** over its symbols), and the market, your mat, the comms log and the score track open from handles on the left edge. With the market closed the map has about two thirds of the screen, where it had about a third. The button beside your hand that converts level I starters is now **Convert all** on every screen. Held upright, the game shows a prompt to turn the phone and waits until you do, without losing your place. A guidance or Learn to play note has one place at the right edge and never covers the map; a step about your mat or the score track opens that drawer. [Playing with friends](rulebook.html#shared-tables).

Before, a name such as Construct could run under the right-hand corner. [Read a card from its corners](rulebook.html#card-corners).
